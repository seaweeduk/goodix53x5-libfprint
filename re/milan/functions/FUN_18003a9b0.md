## Behavior

The function builds a 4096-bin histogram from interior residual values below
`4095`, accumulates from the high end, and selects the first level whose upper
tail exceeds `rows * columns / 50`. It stores that level in `threshold_out`.

Pixels at least `threshold * 11 / 10` are then examined with a 3x3 neighborhood.
The nine roughness values come from `FUN_18003aff0`, which clamps each requested
center independently to interior `x/y` coordinates. When their lower median
from `FUN_180039c10` satisfies `median * 5 <= center * 2`, this function builds a
second nine-value set directly around the original flat residual pointer and
replaces the pixel with its lower median. This replacement load does not reuse
the clamped center: its offsets are exactly `[-columns-1, -columns,
-columns+1, -1, +1, +columns-1, +columns, +columns+1, 0]`. Interior column-zero
pixels therefore take their left neighbors from the preceding flat row, and
interior last-column pixels take their right neighbors from the following row.
Other pixels remain unchanged.

The caller passes its extracted chip type (12 on this path) as the fourth
argument. That argument is not read by this body: `R9` is overwritten before use.
The roughness helper sums nine absolute unsigned-word differences, including
the zero self-difference. Its maximum is `8*65535 = 524280`; multiplying a
roughness median by five or center roughness by two remains within a signed
dword. The median selector mutates its nine-dword scratch; the center roughness
is saved before that call at `0x18003abee`. With nine entries the returned
order statistic is index four. Replacement likewise narrows the selected
nonnegative dword to a word, with no change in value.

## Replicated-Edge Dependency

Border replication does not by itself exclude replacement or make its median
independent of unowned words. Consider the exact local predicate with a positive
background `B`, an isolated inner-top-row value `H>B`, its copied outer-top-row
value `H`, and otherwise background neighbors. Let `D=H-B`. For a column away
from the side edges, all three requested roughness rows clamp to row1; the
nine roughness values are `[2D,7D,2D]` repeated three times. Their median is
`2D`, so the replacement test `5*median <= 2*center` holds for every `D>0`.

When the precomputed histogram selects `B`, `B>=10`, and
`H>=floor(11*B/10)`, preceding background pixels are skipped and the impulse
reaches that predicate without prior mutation. The outer-row replacement
loads four owned `B` words, two owned `H` words and three words before the
allocation. Three external words at most `B` yield median `B`; three at least
`H` yield median `H`. All loads precede median selection and have no native
ownership validation.

The later inner-row impulse passes replacement under either result: its
replacement neighborhood has a strict majority of `B`. Thus that inner value
becomes `B` in both continuations, while the already-processed outer-row value
is never revisited. There is no final border-replication pass. This establishes
a surviving outer-row dependency, rather than only a temporary read difference.

`FUN_180039fc0` includes an outer-row value in its mean whenever the dense mask
there is nonzero, and its radius-five high-pass neighborhoods can read that
value even for an interior selected center. If selected population is `N`,
changing one selected outer value by `D>N/2` from an otherwise constant
residual necessarily changes the rounded mean. This is a conditional consumer
contract: the foreground mask and processed image have their own producers;
raw admission alone does not establish their joint support. See
`FUN_18003a150.md` for raw/setup bounds and `FUN_180039fc0.md` for exact scalar
selection and rounding.

### Bounded Nonconstant Background

The dependency does not require constant neighboring values. Let every other
residual lie in `[m,M]`, with `0<=m<=M`, let the fixed cutoff exceed `M`, and
let the isolated row1 impulse and its copied row0 value be `H`, away from side
edges. If `H>=11*M` and the impulse reaches the cutoff, preceding pixels are
skipped. At each of the six side-column roughness centers there are at most
two `H` samples, giving roughness at most `2*H+6*M`. The center roughness is at
least `7*(H-M)`. Thus
`5*(2*H+6*M)<=2*7*(H-M)` suffices for the native replacement predicate.

The outer replacement has four owned words in `[m,M]`, two owned `H` words,
and three unowned words. External words at most `m` produce a result in
`[m,M]`; external words at least `H` produce `H`. The same roughness bound
admits the later inner impulse in both continuations, and its replacement
median lies in `[m,M]` because at most two of its nine words exceed `M`.
All other pixels stay below the fixed cutoff and are skipped. The only
possible final differences are therefore the surviving outer impulse and an
inner-word difference of at most `M-m`.

For a fixed dense mask selecting the outer impulse, the difference between
the two selected residual sums is at least `H-2*M+m`, whether or not the
inner impulse is also selected. If this exceeds the selected population, the
rounded mean necessarily differs. This bound does not assume that the
preprocessing mask survives an arbitrary raw change: it applies after the
same-call producer has supplied that mask.

The surviving outer word is a real builder-owned output dependency on the
admitted type-12 path. A replicated 65535 impulse can remain 65535 at row 0
after the inner row 1 impulse becomes 974; the later consumers read that owned
post-filter word without another border repair. A separately allocated or
stack-backed replay of the filter need not produce the builder's filtered
plane, even for identical declared residual inputs: the three allocation-end
loads refer to different unowned storage. Complete-builder observation must
therefore capture its own residual owner rather than substitute such a replay.

## What Action 4 Achieves

Profile 9 permits 40 active learned features. Below that limit, a useful accepted
probe can be appended. At exactly 40 features, action 4 is the primary
retained-evidence replacement path: it keeps the template bounded while adapting
its stored views to successful real use.

The policy first rejects an ineligible or markedly inferior probe. It then seeks
an active nonreference feature whose mask has the least unique residual coverage
after the other active views are removed. Lower coverage and greater overlap
break later ranking ties. The probe must supply enough currently uncovered
footprint to justify evicting that feature. Semantically, action 4 attempts to
trade a redundant stored pose for a newly useful pose rather than merely retain
the most recent touch or the feature that produced the match.

The mutation replaces the chosen target's probe-owned records, masks,
quality/coverage, and anti-fake state, preserves or advances target- and
matched-owned lifecycle fields according to their separate contracts, installs
the matcher-owned relation to the graph reference, recomputes gallery residual
coverage, finalizes order and counters, and publishes a positive persistent
candidate. The recognition-selected feature and the action-4 eviction target are
different roles. A statement that matching selected feature 12 or 25 does not by
itself identify the feature slot replaced by study.

Action 4 therefore implements bounded continual learning with graph continuity:
retain identity, broaden useful pose/contact representation, remove redundancy,
and avoid unbounded template growth.
